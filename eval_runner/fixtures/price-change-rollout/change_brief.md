# Kestrelwick price change: the brief

Kestrelwick sells online booking, card payments and class reminders to yoga and fitness studios. On March 1, 2027 its prices go up, one plan gets a new name and the free trial gets longer. All 14 assets in assets.md go live on March 1, 2027, so each must read as it should from that day. Update every asset to this brief: change everything it changes, and nothing else.

## 1. Prices

| Plan | Monthly until February 28, 2027 | Monthly from March 1, 2027 | Yearly until February 28, 2027 | Yearly from March 1, 2027 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solo | $19/month | $24/month | $190/year | $240/year |
| Studio, renamed Growth | $49/month | $59/month | $490/year | $590/year |
| Multi-site | $99/month | $119/month | $990/year | $1,190/year |

A yearly plan costs 10 times the monthly price, before and after the change. The copy also states two figures worked out from the prices. Work each one out again from the new prices:

- **What paying yearly saves** on a plan: 12 times its monthly price, minus its yearly price.
- **The difference between two plans**: the higher monthly price minus the lower.

Every price, yearly price, saving and difference in the copy changes to its new figure, however the old copy words it, with one exception: the sentence for existing customers in section 4.

## 2. The plan's new name

The Studio plan is now called Growth. Change the name wherever it names the plan: in a heading, a subject line, a list, the middle of a sentence, and a possessive (Studio's becomes Growth's). As the plan's name, Studio always starts with a capital S. Leave these alone, though they contain the same letters:

- `studio` and `studios` in lower case. They mean a customer's business, not the plan.
- `StudioSync`, the calendar sync feature. It keeps its name.
- `kestrelwick.example/plans/studio`. The address keeps working after the change and stays exactly as it is.

## 3. The free trial

The free trial is now 30 days. It was 14 days. Change every statement of its length, however the old copy writes it (`14 days`, `14-day`, `two weeks`, `two-week`, `Two weeks` at the start of a sentence), to the same thing in days: `30 days` where the copy says how long (`free for 30 days`, `30 days free`), and `30-day` where the length describes the trial (`a 30-day free trial`).

## 4. Existing customers keep their price until August 31, 2027

Customers who subscribed before March 1, 2027 keep the price they pay today until August 31, 2027, and pay the new price from September 1, 2027. One asset is written to existing customers: asset-05, sent to customers on the plan being renamed. In it, the sentence that tells the reader what they pay becomes this sentence, word for word:

Because you subscribed before March 1, 2027, your price stays at $49/month until August 31, 2027.

That is the only place an old price may appear once the change is made, and it is not a price you changed. Every other price in asset-05 is the price of a plan change or a new subscription, and takes its new figure.

## 5. The notice

Four assets end with this sentence, word for word, as a paragraph of its own after everything else in the asset: the pricing page section (asset-01), the FAQ (asset-02), the sales one-pager (asset-12) and the help article (asset-13).

Prices are in US dollars and apply to new subscriptions from March 1, 2027; current subscribers keep their price until August 31, 2027.

It appears once in each of those four and in no other asset: no email, no ad, not the banner.

## 6. How a price is written

Write every price you change as `$59/month` or `$590/year`: a dollar sign, whole dollars with a comma for thousands (`$1,190/year`), no cents, then `/month` or `/year` with no space before it. This replaces every way the old copy writes a price (`$49 a month`, `$49 per month`, `$49/mo`, `$490 a year`, `$490 per year`), and the words that wrote it (`a month`, `per year`) go with it. A saving or a difference is a dollar amount alone (`$40`), and the words around it stay as they are.

In the six ads and the in-app banner, and only there, `/mo` may stand for `/month` and `/yr` for `/year`. It is the only shortening allowed.

## 7. Limits

An ad has two fields, each on a line of its own: `Headline:` at most 40 characters and `Body:` at most 125 characters. The in-app banner has `Text:`, at most 90 characters, and `Link:`, which has no limit. A limit counts every character after the label and the one space that follows it, spaces and punctuation included. Keep the labels as they are.

## 8. What does not change

- Only the prices, yearly prices, savings and differences of section 1, the plan's name and the trial's length change. A number that is not one of those stays as it is, even when it equals an old price: Kestrelwick connects to 49 integrations, before and after. The card rate, 2.9% plus 25 cents per booking, does not change.
- Every other word stays exactly as it is: no rewording, no new sentences, no tidying, and no shortening but the one in section 6.
- An asset with nothing in it that this brief changes comes back exactly as it is, character for character.

## 9. The answer block

- A1 is the number of assets whose text you changed.
- A2 is the number of amounts of money in the 14 assets that now show a different figure. Count each written amount once. The price in the sentence of section 4 is the same figure as before, so it does not count.
- A3 is the new yearly price of the renamed plan, in dollars.
